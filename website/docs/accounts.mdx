# Accounts

An account is anything that holds money or debt: a checking account, a credit card, cash in
your wallet, a loan, a retirement account or your home. Every transaction belongs to one
account.

<Screenshot name="accounts" alt="The Accounts page" />

The **Accounts** page shows your net worth over time, your assets and liabilities by kind, and
every open account grouped by type, with its balance and how that group changed over the last
month. The sidebar shows the same balances: **All accounts** is your net worth, **For budget** adds
up the accounts your budget uses, and **Off budget** the rest.
[Accounts in another currency](#accounts-in-another-currency) count at the latest exchange rate.

## Adding an account

Choose **Add Account** (on the Accounts page, or **Add account** in the sidebar), then enter:

- **Account Name**, like "Chase Checking".
- **Account Type** (see below).
- **Currency**, if the account is in a different currency from your budget's (see
  [Accounts in another currency](#accounts-in-another-currency)).
- **Current Balance** as of today. For a credit card or loan, enter what you owe as a positive
  number; FlyBudget records it as a debt.
- **Off budget**, ticked for you on investments, property and loans (see below).

<Screenshot name="add-account" alt="The Add Account dialog" dialog />

To create accounts from your bank instead, [connect a bank](./bank-sync): FlyBudget adds its
accounts for you, or links them to accounts you already have.

## Account types

| Group       | Types                                         | On budget by default |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Cash        | Checking, savings, cash                       | Yes                  |
| Credit      | Credit card, line of credit                   | Yes                  |
| Investments | Investment, retirement, crypto                | No                   |
| Property    | Real estate, vehicle, valuables               | No                   |
| Loans       | Mortgage, auto loan, student loan, other loan | No                   |
| Other       | Other asset, other liability                  | No                   |

Credit cards, lines of credit, loans and other liabilities are **debts**: their balances are
negative and count against your net worth.

## On budget and off budget

Money in **on-budget** accounts is money you plan with: its income counts toward
[To Be Budgeted](./budgeting#to-be-budgeted) and its spending shows up in your categories.
**Off-budget** accounts (investments, property, loans) still count toward your net worth, but
their transactions stay out of the budget. Change it for any account with the **Off budget**
box in its **Edit** dialog, including [accounts in another currency](#accounts-in-another-currency).

Money can still move between the two. A [transfer](./transactions#transfers) between an on-budget
account and an off-budget one counts in your budget when its on-budget side has a category.

## Accounts in another currency

Most accounts are in your budget's currency and don't need anything set. For an account in a
different currency, choose its currency when you add the account, or in its **Edit** dialog. Your
budget's currency is set in [Settings → Preferences](./settings#preferences).

FlyBudget converts these accounts' money into your budget's currency at an exchange rate:

- **Each transaction is converted at its day's rate, and keeps it.** When rates move later, past
  months stay as they were. Money [exchanged](./transactions#exchanges) into your budget's currency
  is converted at exactly what arrived.
- **Balances use the latest rate.** The account shows its balance in its own currency, and next to
  it what that's worth in your budget's currency. Your net worth counts it the same way, and each
  month of the [net worth report](./reports) uses that month's rate.
- **Rates come from [Settings → Currencies](./settings#currencies).** FlyBudget can download the
  European Central Bank's daily rates, if you turn that on, or you can enter rates yourself.
- **A transaction without a rate isn't counted yet.** It stays out of your budget and reports
  until there's a rate for its day, and the budget tells you how many are waiting.

You decide for each account whether it's in your budget:

- **Off budget** is where a new account in another currency starts. Its transactions stay out of
  your budget, and its money counts once you exchange it into your budget's currency (see
  [Income in another currency](#income-in-another-currency)). Income and spending reports still
  include its categorized transactions, converted.
- **On budget** works like any other budget account. Its income adds to
  [To Be Budgeted](./budgeting#to-be-budgeted) and its spending counts in your categories,
  converted at each day's rate. Choose this if you spend from the account.

Moving an account on or off budget removes the category from transfers between it and your other
accounts that no longer cross the budget boundary, so the same money isn't counted twice. The
**Edit** dialog warns you first.

### Converting a transaction yourself

Open a transaction in an account in another currency to see what it's worth in your budget's
currency (**Value in EUR**, for a euro budget) and where that came from: the day's rate, an
exchange, or an amount you entered. If you were charged a different amount, for example a card
payment your bank converted at its own rate, type that amount there instead. **Use the day's rate**
goes back.

### Changing your budget's currency

When you change your budget's currency in [Settings → Preferences](./settings#preferences),
accounts already in the new currency become the budget's own. If you have accounts in the old
currency, FlyBudget asks which currency their amounts are in:

- **They're in the new currency.** Choose this when the first currency was wrong, for example if
  your budget started in dollars but your accounts were always in euros. Only the name of the
  budget's currency changes; amounts stay as they are. Exchange rates you typed in for other
  currencies need typing in again (or downloading) for the new one.
- **They stay in the old currency.** They become accounts in another currency, and no balance
  changes. FlyBudget converts them again at the new rates, and amounts you entered by hand for
  conversions are cleared, because they were in the old currency.

Either way, amounts you've assigned to categories keep their numbers, now in the new currency, so
check your budget afterwards.

## Income in another currency

Say you're paid every week in pounds (GBP) into a GBP account at Revolut, and your budget is in
euros (EUR). There are two ways to budget it. Pick the one that matches how you use the money.

### You change the pounds into euros before spending them

Keep the GBP account **off budget**. The pay counts for your budget once it reaches your euro
account, exactly what you received:

1. Add the Revolut account in GBP, with its currency set to GBP.
2. When you change pounds into euros in Revolut, that's an exchange. FlyBudget records it as a
   transfer between the two accounts: the GBP side keeps the pounds that left, and the euro side
   keeps the euros that arrived.
3. Give the euro side an income category, such as **Salary**. The euros now count toward To Be
   Budgeted. To do this automatically, add a [rule](./rules) that sets the category when the bank's
   original text contains `Exchanged from GBP`.

If both accounts come from the same bank connection, FlyBudget can recognize the exchange from the
bank's text. Otherwise, link the two sides yourself with **Link as transfer** (see
[Linking by hand](./transactions#linking-by-hand)).

### You also spend from the pound account

Put the GBP account **on budget**. Each week's pay then counts toward To Be Budgeted when it
arrives, converted at that day's rate, and what you pay from the account counts in your categories,
converted the same way. Changing pounds into euros is then a transfer between two budget accounts,
so it doesn't change your budget.

The budget keeps the pay at the rate of the day it arrived. If you later change the pounds at a
better or worse rate, the difference isn't counted. If that matters to you, add an adjustment for it
with a category.

To keep track of the weekly pay itself, add it as a [recurring transaction](./recurring#pay-in-another-currency)
in the GBP account.

## An account's register

Click an account to open its register: its balance, type and every transaction in it. From the
header you can:

- **Add Transaction** or **Import CSV** (see [Transactions](./transactions) and
  [Importing transactions](./importing)).
- **Update value** (or **Update balance** for a debt) for investments, property and loans.
- **Reconcile** it against a bank statement.
- **Edit** it.

## Updating the value of an investment or property

Homes, cars and investment accounts usually don't have transactions worth entering one by one.
Choose **Update value**, enter what it's worth today, and FlyBudget adds a dated adjustment for
the difference, so your net worth history stays right.

<Screenshot name="update-value" alt="Updating the value of a car" dialog />

Adjustments (from **Update value** or from reconciling) change the balance and your net worth,
but they aren't money earned or spent, so income and spending reports leave them out. If you
give one a category, it counts like any other transaction.

## Reconciling

Reconciling checks that FlyBudget agrees with your bank. Do it when a statement arrives, or
whenever the balances look different.

1. Open the account and choose **Reconcile**.
2. Enter the **Statement Balance** from your statement or online banking, and choose
   **Start Reconciliation**.
3. Tick each transaction that appears on the statement. FlyBudget shows the balance of what
   you've ticked and the **Difference** from the statement.
4. When the difference is zero, choose **Finish**. If you can't find what's missing, choose
   **Create Adjustment** instead: FlyBudget adds a reconciliation adjustment for the difference.

<Screenshot name="reconcile" alt="Reconciling an account" />

Reconciled transactions get a lock icon and can't be edited or deleted, so rules and bank sync
never change them.

## Editing and closing an account

**Edit** changes the name, type, currency, starting balance and whether it's off budget, and lets
you add a **logo** (a PNG, JPEG or WebP image, cropped to a square) in place of the colored
initials.
You can hide account logos everywhere in **Settings → Preferences → Icons**.

**Close Account** (at the bottom of the Edit dialog) takes it off the sidebar and the Accounts
page. Its transactions stay, so past reports and your net worth history remain correct.

To change the order accounts appear in, use **Settings → Accounts**.
